## Options

- **A. Ratify §2.9's reading (recommended).** Rule that S3's escalation clause attaches
  to the final Result: a transient FAIL whose cause is removed by the owning lane's own
  legitimate commits before the final record — with no workaround by the checking
  session and the full sequence documented — does not require an escalation. Optionally
  record a one-line precedent in `docs/decisions.md` so future pre-flights have it.
  **Cost:** one decision line. **Risk:** low, if the ruling is scoped to exactly these
  conditions (transient, externally resolved by the owning lane, no workaround, final
  Result all-PASS, sequence documented).
- **B. Require a retroactive escalation brief for the observed P8 FAIL.** T-019 files a
  brief listing the FAIL (already fully documented in §2.8); the planning chat notes it
  with no pending action, since the cause is gone. **Cost:** one more brief + decision
  round-trip for an already-resolved state; sets the precedent that ANY observed FAIL
  blocks a pre-flight even when another lane resolves it seconds later. **Risk:** low,
  but future pre-flights stop on transient states they cannot control.

## Recommendation

**Option A**, with the one-line precedent recorded in `docs/decisions.md`. The FAIL's
cause was another lane's legitimate work, committed by that lane itself; T-019 applied
no workaround; the final record is clean and fully documented; and the reviewer
independently verified every fact. **Confidence:** high on the facts (re-derived by the
reviewer); medium-high on A being the intended reading — "escalate once listing every
failure" most naturally reads against the final Result, but `must_escalate: any FAIL`
is genuinely ambiguous, which is why this brief exists.
