# Stage 1 diagnostics: memory and speed of the windows that paged

> SCREENING DATA (2 repetitions). Descriptive and anecdotal: one run per cell and repetition, one machine state, desktop apps open (G3 not met). No causal claim beyond what the numbers show.
> Tables below are generated by `uv run python tools/stage1_diagnostics.py` (read-only: telemetry CSVs, cell summaries, records and server logs). Nothing here changes a flag, an eligibility rule or a statistic. `data/analysis/paging-stage1-64K` and `-128K` (`.json`, `.md`) are the output of `smallpc.paging_analysis`; their banner "Functional-test data (WP9 mini-sweep)" is a stale label in that tool, these are Stage 1 screening data.

## Reading (hypothesis test, with the numbers)

**Hypothesis H.** The paging windows (more than 1,000 major faults, repetition 1 of 14 cells at 128K and of stock-64K) come from RAM pressure that builds as llama-server's host prompt cache (default 8,192 MiB, server RssAnon) fills over successive fresh prompts, on top of about 20 GiB of mmapped model pages (RssFile) held by the cells whose expert tensors stay in host memory.

**Log line used for the host prompt cache size:** `srv update: - cache state: N prompts, X MiB (limits: 8192.000 MiB, 131072 tokens, ...)`, written when a fresh request makes the server save the idle slot's prompt (`-lv 4`); for example line 417 of `data/logs/stage1-128K-P3-128K-b2048-ec9defe0-i0/server.log` (`cache state: 1 prompts, 274.948 MiB`). Log times are aligned to the wall clock through the `slot print_timing ... total time` line of each request. The value at the last save of a window omits the slot that is still active (up to about 1.4 GiB at 96K).

What matches H (128K, 14 flagged cells; 14 attempt-2 windows; 4 P8/P9 repetition-1 controls):

| | RssAnon at window start (MiB) | host cache at last save (MiB) | RssAnon + VmSwap at end minus host cache (MiB) | MemAvailable min (GiB) | change of RssFile (MiB) | major faults | VmSwap max (MiB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| flagged windows, repetition 1, attempt 1 (n = 14) | 3,425 to 4,837 (median 4,365) | 5,666 to 6,884 (median 6,007) | 823 to 1,008 | 17.8 to 19.7 | -8,051 to -1,454 (median -2,365) | 1,077 to 81,062 (median 8,712) | 3,011 to 5,400 |
| repetition 0 of the same cells (n = 14) | 1,031 to 1,292 (median 1,196) | 3,794 to 4,263 | -532 to 852 | 21.3 to 22.2 | -78 to 0 | 0 to 97 | 0 to 823 |
| attempt 2 of the same units, fresh server (n = 14) | 1,170 to 1,292 (median 1,226) | 2,422 to 4,903 (median 2,763) | 814 to 1,018 | 20.4 to 22.6 | -761 to 0 | 0 to 327 | 0 to 2,366 |

1. The anonymous memory of the server at the start of a repetition-1 window is about 3.2 GiB higher than on a fresh server, and the log shows 3,794 MiB (6 prompts; stock 4,263 MiB, 5 prompts) in the host cache at that point.
2. At the end of every repetition-1 and attempt-2 window (32 of 32), RssAnon + VmSwap equals the host cache at its last save plus 0.81 to 1.02 GiB. So the growth of the anonymous footprint is accounted for by the cache to within about 0.2 GiB in those windows (a roughly constant offset). In repetition-0 windows this offset is not constant (-0.5 to +0.85 GiB); I cannot say why.
3. Over all 46 windows with both series the least-squares slope of footprint growth on cache growth is 0.94 MiB per MiB, but Pearson r is only 0.44: every repetition-0 window of the non-stock cells has the same cache growth (3,244 MiB) while its footprint growth ranges from 1.8 to 3.3 GiB (lower at `-b 4096`), so the correlation is weak where the cache growth does not vary.
4. The flagged windows have the lowest MemAvailable (17.8 to 19.7 GiB, against 21.3 to 22.2 in repetition 0 of the same cells), every one of them lost 1.5 to 8.1 GiB of RssFile (the kernel reclaimed mapped model pages) and read part of it back (the major faults), and swapped out 3.0 to 5.4 GiB of anonymous memory.
5. Attempt-2 windows, which start with an empty host cache, had a host cache of 2,422 to 4,903 MiB at their last save, 14 of 14 raised no flag (median faults 0).

What does not fit, or is not decided:
- **Controls.** P8 and P9 (explicit `--n-cpu-moe`, RssFile 8 to 10 GiB) have the same cache growth, the same footprint (6.7 to 7.3 GiB) and the same low MemAvailable (18.6 to 19.2 GiB) as the flagged cells, and still stayed under 1,000 faults; P8-128K-b2048 swapped out 2,277 MiB with 61 faults, P9-128K-b2048 none. The difference of about 10 GiB of resident model pages fits H but was not tested: these cells also differ in placement.
- **P1-128K-b4096** (unflagged, RssFile 19,968 MiB, footprint 7,187 MiB, MemAvailable min 17.9 GiB, lower than the flagged cells) lost 6.9 GiB of RssFile with 20 faults and no swap. Low MemAvailable plus reclaim of mapped pages does not by itself give faults or swap.
- **64K** (section D): the same picture in the six windows with swap growth above 64 MiB (all repetition 1; host cache 4.1 to 4.5 GiB at the last save, 6.3 GiB for stock-64K; slope 0.83, r 0.76), but P8-64K-b4096 swapped out 2,665 MiB with 5 faults and only 8.8 GiB of RssFile, so swap-out also happens without the large mapping; the 1,000-fault flag goes with RssFile 20 GiB in these data.
- **Not shown by these data:** causation (nothing was varied: `--cache-ram` and the free memory were not manipulated); which pages were swapped (VmSwap is the server's own, other processes are not attributed); what the other desktop apps held at each moment; whether the repetition-1 effect is the cache or the server's uptime (screening has no repetition 2 or later); and whether the clean attempt-2 windows owe it to the empty cache or to the time of day (the re-runs ran 08:57 to 09:48 UTC against 01:22 to 03:33 UTC, with different desktop activity). Sampling is 2 Hz and the figures are single runs.

**Verdict on H:** the numbers are consistent with H (the anonymous growth is the host prompt cache; the windows that paged are those where it had accumulated across repetitions and the 20 GiB mapping was resident) and do not contradict it; they do not establish it.

**Re-run units (section E).** 15 of 15 attempt-2 units are clean (1 at 64K, 14 at 128K). Compared with attempt 1 of the same repetition, attempt 2 had median 0 faults (maximum 327, stock-128K) and a host cache of 2.4 to 3.1 GiB (stock-128K 4.9 GiB) at its last save instead of 5.7 to 6.9 GiB; one attempt-2 window, stock-128K, still grew VmSwap by 2,366 MiB (327 faults, below the flag threshold), the largest cache of the set. This supports H and does not contradict it. Mean T_turn_400 is 0.9 % lower in attempt 2 (-5.0 % to +1.1 %), mostly in the expert-cache cells (P5-b2048 -5.0 %, P7-b4096 -4.2 %, P7-b2048 -2.4 %, P6-b4096 -1.8 %), which is the size and sign of the ordinary repetition-1 against repetition-0 difference of unflagged cache cells in section C (+1.9 % T_turn_400 in repetition 1).

**Speed effect (section C, anecdotal).** The flagged windows are not visibly slower than the baseline of ordinary repetition-to-repetition differences. Flagged 128K cells, repetition 1 against 0: PP_fresh -0.7 % (range -2.2 to +0.2), TG -1.7 % (-9.7 to +3.0), T_turn_400 +1.3 % (-2.2 to +6.4). Unflagged cells of 32K and 64K (37 cells): PP_fresh -0.4 %, TG -1.2 %, T_turn_400 +1.1 % (-1.0 to +7.8); the 5 unflagged 128K cells: 0.0 %, 0.0 %, -0.3 %. Split by type, no-cache cells: flagged -0.1 % (4 cells) against +0.1 % (17); cache cells (P3 to P7): +1.9 % (10) against +1.9 % (20). The largest slow-downs (P5, P6, P7: TG down 5 to 15 %) occur in the same cell types, with the same size, in the unflagged 32K and 64K sessions.
